## 1. Executive Research Summary

**What this source contributes uniquely.** *Daniels' Running Formula* is fundamentally a physiological-prescription system, not a philosophical or narrative work — its value to the Haarchive is different in kind from the *Running with the Buffaloes* synthesis that preceded this one. Where Buffaloes contributed texture, psychology, and honest cost/benefit narrative from one program's real season, Daniels contributes:

1. **A precise, named vocabulary and quantitative framework for workout purpose** (E/M/T/I/R, the "two structural shapes" of steady vs. intermittent training, explicit physiological reasoning for I-pace work-bout duration) that meaningfully sharpens the site's existing workout-purpose content, most of which is currently sourced from Livingstone/Fitzgerald/Buchheit & Laursen rather than Daniels himself, despite Daniels already having a Coaching Library entry built around this exact system.
2. **A real, complete, novel periodization-*planning*-methodology** (the four-phase B/FIP→IQ→TQ→FQ season model, built backward from the goal race, with an explicit, numbered algorithm for compressing it into a shorter available calendar) — genuinely distinct in its *planning process*, not just its phase count, from Lydiard's, Wetmore's, Vigil's, Canova's, Pfitzinger's, and the Norwegian System's periodization content already on the site.
3. **A genuinely important epistemic clarification of what VDOT actually is** (a performance-derived "pseudo VO2max," not a lab measurement) — directly answers this task's own explicit VDOT-research questions and corrects a real, plausible misunderstanding a reader could otherwise carry away from the site's existing VDOT mention.
4. **A real, novel, quantified training-load scoring system** (points per minute by zone) with no clear existing equivalent on the site.
5. **A third, independent, genuinely dissenting voice on altitude training** — Daniels is actively skeptical that altitude explains elite performance at all, a real, quotable complication of the site's existing Vigil (structured-relocation) and newly-added Wetmore ("debatable") perspectives.
6. **Real, concrete, marathon-specific structural content** (per-zone volume caps as percentages of weekly mileage, a table-free pace-differential shortcut, a clean recovery-day formula, and real evidence that elite marathon training doesn't have to be organized around a 7-day week).
7. **A real, complete coaching-practice toolkit** (the 12 Laws of Running, the Runner Profile intake questionnaire, an explicit team-selection philosophy prioritizing broad health over peak individual fitness) independently corroborating several Buffaloes-derived `for-coaches` additions from real, entirely different program.

**What it does NOT contribute, or contributes only redundantly.** A real, disciplined duplication check (detailed in §4 below) found that two of Daniels' most prominent physiological arguments — VO2max's limits as a performance predictor (compensated for by running economy) and the unreliability of age-predicted max heart rate — are **already thoroughly covered on the site**, with their own strong, independently-sourced case studies (Prefontaine/Shorter/Clayton, Radcliffe's 12-year case study, a 37-cyclist max-HR dataset). Per the task's own explicit instruction, these were **not** re-explained with Daniels' own examples layered on top; instead, the single genuinely additive contribution (crediting Daniels' own coined term, vVO2max, as the named framework behind an idea the site already explains well) was integrated narrowly. The book's own extensive white/red/blue/gold generic training plans and full event-specific weekly tables were not reproduced or described in detail, consistent with both the copyright-handling instruction and the fact that the site's own Training Plans tool already serves this exact function architecturally better than a static description of Daniels' tables would.

**Most important new knowledge**: the four-phase backward-built periodization methodology (TR-1 below), the VDOT epistemic clarification (VD-1), the per-zone training-load points system (TL-1), and the three-way altitude disagreement (PH-3).

**Most important contradictions/caveats**: Daniels' own real, quotable altitude skepticism sitting in tension with Vigil's altitude-relocation model (§5), and a genuine, unresolved question about whether Daniels' own strength-training endorsement is as central to his system as the site's existing `strength-training` page might imply (§5).

## 4. Duplication / Contradiction Audit (Phase 4)

This is the single most important disciplined check this extraction performed, per the task's own explicit instruction not to add duplicate explanations.

**Confirmed genuine duplicates, deliberately excluded from integration:**
- **VO2max-vs-running-economy as competing performance predictors.** The site already covers this thoroughly and well in `research-library`'s "VO2 Max Doesn't Decide Who Wins" (Prefontaine/Shorter/Clayton's 15-point VO2max spread at comparable performance; Paula Radcliffe's 12-year longitudinal case) and `exercise-physiology`'s adjacent VO2max content. Daniels' own real, tested examples (two teammates, three elite women) make the identical point with different data. **Not integrated as a re-explanation** — the only addition made is crediting Daniels' own vVO2max term as the named framework this point traces back to (see VD-1/PH-1 in the table above).
- **Age-predicted max heart rate's unreliability.** The site already has a real, concrete 37-trained-cyclist dataset making this exact point. Daniels' own two case examples were **not** added as further illustration of an already-well-evidenced claim. Only a genuinely distinct, complementary practical technique (PH-3, a self-test protocol for finding true max HR) was considered for narrow, optional integration.

**Genuine contradictions/complications, preserved rather than resolved:**
- **Altitude training's actual causal role.** A real, three-way, unresolved disagreement now exists in the site's own content once this synthesis is implemented: Vigil treats altitude as a real, structured training lever requiring a defined relocation/adaptation protocol; Wetmore (per the Buffaloes synthesis) lives and trains at altitude but calls its specific physiological benefit "debatable," and states his actual ideal would be to live high and train lower; Daniels is actively, rhetorically skeptical that altitude explains elite performance at all, challenging the reader to explain why naturally high-altitude South American countries haven't produced disproportionately many elite distance runners. **This is preserved as a genuine, real, three-way disagreement, not resolved in any one direction.**
- **Strength training's centrality.** The site's existing Jack Daniels Coaching Library entry does not currently list strength/resistance training among his `corePrinciples`, but the book itself states in chapter 9 that Daniels "encourage[s] all runners to include resistance training in their weekly program" and provides a full circuit routine.
