## Real, quotable racing-execution content

- **Chapter epigraph, worth a standalone quotable mention**: "You can't control what others do, nor can they control you."
- **A real, clean, quantified racing-execution heuristic, distinct from existing site pacing content**: "the race actually starts about two-thirds of the way through" a given event — in a 5K, be ready to race hard only after the first couple of miles; in a 10K, the real race begins around the 4-mile mark. Up to that point, the actual skill being tested is staying relaxed while still holding the planned pace or position. **A genuinely reusable, standalone racing-psychology/pacing principle: reframes the early portion of a race as primarily a relaxation-and-discipline test, with the real competitive effort deliberately reserved for the final third — complements, without duplicating, the site's existing "bookend" pacing content in `marathon-training` (Racing the Last 25%) by giving 5K/10K their own specific fractions.**
- **A real, named, concrete counter-example to "more volume is always better"**: Kenenisa Bekele's own stated explanation for exceeding Haile Gebrselassie's 5K/10K standards, including a sub-51-second closing lap, credited by Bekele himself to "focused hill and altitude training at a relatively low volume (80 miles per week)." **A real, standalone data point worth flagging — 80 mi/week is genuinely modest by the standards of the elite-volume examples already logged elsewhere in this synthesis (Goucher's 90-100+, Meb's 140-150 per 9-day cycle), and Bekele's own account attributes his results to training quality/terrain (hills, altitude) rather than raw volume — a useful, real complication of any simple "volume wins" narrative, worth pairing with the existing volume-tradeoff content on site.**
