## Episode #246: 9 Truths about Training Paces
**Guest(s) and credentials:** No guest — solo episode, Chris McClung (Rogue Running head coach).

**Concepts & claims:**
- Truth 1: "Pace is a proxy for effort, but effort is still king" — prescribed training paces exist only to approximate a target physiological effort zone; if conditions (heat, altitude) make the pace wrong for the intended effort, effort should win out over hitting the number. Heart rate is cited as a "probably better" proxy than pace, but only if you've done proper testing to know your zones. (SCIENCE — general exercise-physiology claim, no specific study cited; more a coaching principle) (LIKELY EXISTING TOPIC — training-by-effort/zones is likely already covered, but explicit "pace is a proxy, not the goal" framing could sharpen existing content)
- Truth 2: Hitting target paces in a workout is not the same as workout "success" — success is about achieving the right effort/physiological stimulus; a "failed" workout (wrong pace) can still become valuable if the athlete learns from it and applies the lesson later (e.g., progression runs are commonly botched by newer runners but the resulting body-awareness carries forward). (PRACTICE)
- Truth 3: Training paces should be triangulated from multiple data points (time trials, recent races at various distances, current goals, felt effort in workouts) rather than derived from a single time trial plugged into a calculator (McMillan calculator named specifically). Paces should be treated as "written in pencil," tested, and iterated. (PRACTICE) (LIKELY EXISTING TOPIC — race-pace prediction calculators are common knowledge, but the explicit "don't over-trust a single data point" caution is a useful specific angle)
- Truth 4 (flagged by host as "controversial"): "Train where you are, not where you're going" — attributed to a stated agreement from Greg McMillan (referenced from episode 75 interview) — training paces should reflect current fitness, not a stretch goal never yet demonstrated, even if that means training at paces below what a runner's ultimate goal time implies. Overreaching in training pushes you out of the intended effort zone. (PRACTICE/SCIENCE-adjacent, attributed to a named expert McMillan but not a study)
- Truth 5: Use a pace RANGE (roughly ±15-20 sec/mile around a target) rather than a single pinpoint pace for each training zone (5K/10K/half/marathon pace), since a range still corresponds to the correct effort zone and accounts for daily variation (sleep, stress, etc.). Narrow the range only during race-specific phase when dialing into actual race pace. (PRACTICE)
- Truth 6: Must adjust training paces for conditions — altitude, heat, and humidity all shift the pace/effort correlation; cites personal example of paces 20-40 sec/mile slower in Austin heat/humidity for equivalent effort, and states runners must set aside ego about the watch. (SCIENCE — general physiological claim, no study cited, personal/anecdotal support)
- Truth 7: Training paces and race paces are NOT necessarily the same — training paces are chosen to hit effort zones for physiological adaptation, while race pace is a separate decision informed by training outcomes, goals, weather, and course terrain; race pace can end up faster OR slower than corresponding training pace. (PRACTICE) (POSSIBLE GAP — this training-pace/race-pace decoupling is a specific and possibly underappreciated distinction worth flagging if the site's existing content conflates the two)
- Truth 8: Training paces should evolve within and across training cycles based on accumulated evidence (feeling too easy/too hard, fitness gains, injury setbacks); recommends checking in with a coach or training partner for an honest outside perspective since self-assessment is hard. (PRACTICE)
- Truth 9: Training pace progress is NOT linear — expect steps backward (injury, layoffs, weather) as part of an overall upward trajectory; jumping back into old paces too soon after a break is called out explicitly as "a recipe for training in the wrong zones... also a recipe for injury." (PRACTICE) (LIKELY EXISTING TOPIC if injury-return/progressive-overload guidance already exists)
- Closing meta-point: don't let "analysis paralysis" over getting training paces perfect prevent action — pick paces based on available data and gut feel, treat them as adjustable, and iterate. (PRACTICE)
