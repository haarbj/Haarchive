## Episode #270: Super Shoes and Training

(SCIENCE — general claim, no specific study named for the drag mechanism) (POSSIBLE GAP — likely a genuinely new/recent topic for a site built before super shoes existed at scale)
- Cited data point: a study analyzed 10 years (2010-2019) of top-50 men's/women's results across Boston/NY/Chicago/London; times were flat 2010-2017, then dropped starting 2018-19 — average nearly 3 minutes faster for men, just over 4 minutes for women at the front of the field. (SCIENCE — study referenced but not named/sourced with author) (POSSIBLE GAP)
- On the track, elite coaches/athletes report super spikes may be worth 1-2 seconds per lap (~25-50 sec over a 10,000m/25-lap race). (SCIENCE — anecdotal/hearsay from unnamed coaches/athletes) (POSSIBLE GAP)

## Episode #273: The Passion and Occasional Apathy of Running
**Guest(s) and credentials:** No guest — solo episode by host Chris McClung, part of an ongoing personal series on "the emotions of running."

**Concepts & claims:**
- Names five distinct manifestations of apathy in a runner's life: (1) simple day-to-day lack of motivation to get out the door, (2) apathy toward goal-setting itself (not knowing what's next, or having achieved a big goal and lacking a replacement objective), (3) in-race apathy/wanting to quit when things go off-plan (host's own recent example: elevated heart rate and flat legs from mile 1-2 of a goal 10K, wanting to walk off rather than fight for a diminished result), (4) burnout — an intellectual/logical connection to goals that physical and/or mental exhaustion prevents you from acting on (host cites his own two-month sabbatical from Rogue/podcast/coaching in late 2020 for this reason), (5) apathy toward others via comparison/envy ("comparison is the thief of joy") undermining one's own motivation. (PRACTICE — personal framework, not sourced from external psych literature) (POSSIBLE GAP — if the site's sports-psych content doesn't already taxonomize apathy/motivation-loss this granularly, this five-part breakdown is a specific, reusable framework)
- Five antidotes to apathy offered as a non-exhaustive "menu": (1) name/acknowledge the feeling explicitly, (2) "mix it up" — take a break, try a new discipline/route/workout to follow curiosity, (3) reevaluate the goal — sometimes apathy stems from a goal that isn't big/scary enough to inspire the necessary effort (goal should sit in the zone between "I think I can do this" and "I have no idea if I can do this"), (4) just start — cites Brad Stulberg (performance coach/author) on the principle that "motivation follows action" rather than the reverse — take the smallest possible step (e.g., commit only to the first mile) to bootstrap momentum, (5) lean on your support network/team/coach/family rather than retreating inward. (PRACTICE, with one named source — Brad Stulberg) (POSSIBLE GAP — "motivation follows action" as an explicit, named psychological principle with a citable author may be a useful, fact-checkable addition if not already covered)
- Six manifestations of passion in running are named for symmetry: passion of training, passion of racing, passion of finishing, passion of spectating, passion of talking about the sport, passion of being a support person for someone else's journey. (PRACTICE/opinion, largely descriptive) (LIKELY EXISTING TOPIC in general terms, though this specific six-way taxonomy might be new)
- Three ways to "channel" passion productively so it doesn't just dissipate: (1) write it down/journal it (the claim being that humans disproportionately remember low/dim emotional moments over bright ones, so deliberately recording positive moments counteracts negativity bias), (2) "pay it forward" by sharing enthusiasm with others (invite a friend to run, encourage someone to sign up for a race), (3) channel the passion into unglamorous process work (the extra strength session, the foam-rolling, the recovery run you'd otherwise skip). (PRACTICE) (POSSIBLE GAP — the "write down high moments to counteract negativity bias" idea, if not covered, could connect well with existing sports-psych content on gratitude journaling)
