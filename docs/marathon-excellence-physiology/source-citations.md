## Verification log (this session, via WebSearch)

**§A — "Lactic acid" myth correction / lactate shuttle**: confirmed via [The Science and Translation of Lactate Shuttle Theory](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1550413118301864) and [Cell–cell and intracellular lactate shuttles — Brooks, J Physiol 2009](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1113/jphysiol.2009.178350).

**§B — Fat vs. carbohydrate ATP yield per oxygen**: confirmed via [Physiology, Respiratory Quotient — StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK531494/) and [Respiratory Quotient — ScienceDirect overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/respiratory-quotient). RQ carbohydrate = 1.0, fat ≈ 0.7; classic thermal-equivalent-of-oxygen figures (~5.05 vs. ~4.69 kcal/L O2) work out to ~7% — matches Davis's figure closely. Well-established, pre-dates Davis by over a century (Zuntz-era physiology).

**§C — ~75% lactate oxidation**: confirmed via search results describing Brooks/Miller-era tracer studies (treadmill running, [14C]lactate infusion) finding ~75–80% of lactate disposal during exercise is via oxidation, vs. ~50% at rest. Original foundational work was in rats; broader human lactate-shuttle literature corroborates the pattern qualitatively. Davis's specific application of "~75%" to human marathon-intensity running is a reasonable extrapolation, not itself a directly-cited marathon-specific human measurement found in this pass.

**§D — Max HR decreasing with training**: confirmed via [Evidence and Possible Mechanisms of Altered Maximum Heart Rate With Endurance Training and Tapering](https://link.springer.com/article/10.2165/00007256-200029010-00002) (a real, known review on exactly this topic) and correlational findings that HRmax reduction tracks VO2max improvement, mechanistically tied to increased stroke volume/cardiac efficiency. The specific "3–7%" figure was not independently located in this pass's search results.

**§E — vVO2max protocol-dependence**: confirmed via a real, specific paper, [Effect of Protocol on Determination of Velocity at VO2max and on its Time to Exhaustion](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8793023/), directly on this question, plus corroborating findings on optimal test duration (8–12 min) and protocol-dependent VO2max determination.

**§F — Three intensity domains (moderate/heavy/severe)**: confirmed as a real, standard exercise-physiology framework (Whipp/Jones/Poole tradition) via [Exercise Intensity — ScienceDirect overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/immunology-and-microbiology/exercise-intensity) and [Critical Power: An Important Fatigue Threshold in Exercise Physiology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5070974/). Note: some literature uses a *four*-domain model (splitting out "very heavy" between MLSS and VO2max); Davis's three-domain model is a defensible simplification of this, not the only valid taxonomy.

**§G — Ultramarathon central fatigue (electrical stimulation paradigm)**: confirmed as a real, established methodology via [Electrical stimulation for testing neuromuscular function](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00421-011-1996-y) and [Alterations of Neuromuscular Function after an Extreme Mountain Ultra-Marathon](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0017059) — very likely part of the Millet et al. research line (e.g. "Alterations of neuromuscular function after an ultramarathon," Journal of Applied Physiology, 2002), though the exact "25%" figure Davis cites wasn't pinned to one specific paper this pass.

**§H — Jones's 2023 resilience paper**: confirmed as a real, primary, peer-reviewed paper — Jones, "The Fourth Dimension: Physiological Resilience as an Independent Determinant of Endurance Exercise Performance," *Journal of Physiology* (2023, print issue 602.17, 2024). Confirms the core concept (resilience as an independent fourth determinant, athletes with similar starting physiology can diverge 5% vs. 15% in decline over a race) but the specific 96%/75–85% SSmax-sustained figures Davis cites were not independently checked against the paper's own text this pass — worth doing before using them as hard numbers in production.

**§I — 2018 identical-twin fiber-type study**: confirmed as a real, published case study, very likely from California State University Fullerton / San Francisco State (Bathgate et al.) — [source PDF](https://hhd.fullerton.edu/knes/_resources/pdfs/research-lab/bmep/bathgate%202018%20Monozygous%20Twins%20Study.pdf). Exact journal of publication not pinned down with full certainty this pass — confirm before citing a specific journal name in production.

**§J — External vs. internal attentional focus (motor learning)**: confirmed as a very well-established, extensive research program — Gabriele Wulf (UNLV), 15+ years of research, a real theoretical framework (Constrained Action Hypothesis) and integration into mainstream motor-learning theory (OPTIMAL theory). Source: [Attentional focus and motor learning: a review of 15 years — Wulf](https://gwulf.faculty.unlv.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Wulf_AF_review_2013.pdf). This confirms the *general* principle solidly; it does **not** independently confirm Davis's specific application of it to running-economy training, which Davis himself flags as resting on thin evidence (one small, uncontrolled 1999 study) — that distinction must be preserved in any production use.

**§K — Triad-junction glycogen / calcium-pumping fatigue mechanism**: confirmed as real, specific, published muscle physiology — localized myofibrillar glycogen near the triad junction/sarcoplasmic reticulum plays a direct role in calcium release during fatigue, distinct from whole-muscle glycogen depletion. Sources: [Role of glycogen availability in sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ kinetics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21135051/), [Skeletal Muscle Fatigue: Cellular Mechanisms](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/physrev.00015.2007). Very likely part of the Ørtenblad research group's work on this exact topic, though exact primary-paper attribution wasn't pinned down with full certainty this pass.

**§L — Muscle-localized central fatigue**: confirmed via a real, specific, directly-on-point study — [Fatigue is specific to working muscles: no cross-over with single-leg cycling](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22806085/) — finding that neuromuscular fatigue is specific to the working muscles and does not meaningfully cross over to unused muscle groups, supporting Davis's leg-vs-arm and single-leg-cycling claims. This is very likely the exact study (or one of the exact studies) Davis is referencing.
