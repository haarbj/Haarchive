# Digest 01 — Front matter + book pp. 1-15

## Roster / program context (front matter, unpaginated)

- **Mark Wetmore** bio: Rutgers grad (English education, 1978), master's in movement science from Columbia (1988). Started coaching in 1972 (Edge City Track Club). 14 years as assistant to his own high school mentor Ed Mather at Bernards (NJ). 4-year stint as distance coach at Seton Hall. Emigrated to Colorado 1991, named men's distance coach 1992, CU teams finished no lower than 5th at NCAA's every year since 1993, named full head XC/T&F coach (men's and women's) Nov 6, 1995. Had not missed a day of running in over 19 years as of 1998 (i.e., since ~1979) — **verify precision before using "19 years" as a hard fact; it's the book's own claim, treat as book-reported, not independently confirmed.**
- 1998 team enters season with 1997's 3rd-place NCAA finish, losing only 2x All-American Clint Wells.
- Full roster with Wetmore's own preseason assessment quotes + each runner's exact 1997 result (time/place) — usable as *evidence for individualization* (radically different starting points: Adam Goucher, defending 4th-place NCAA finisher, 3x NCAA champion in track, vs. Oscar Ponce, a 101st-place freshman-turned-varsity walk-on from an inner-city Denver high school with no running pedigree, vs. Matt Napier, "one of the great blue-collar success stories," a former walk-on who became a 39th-place All-American).
- Notable individual notes:
  - **Adam Goucher**: senior, Colorado Springs (Doherty). 3x NCAA champion (indoor 3000m collegiate record 7:46.03, outdoor 5000m, 1997 3000m). 1997 XC: undefeated all fall, then felled by a cold the week of NCAA's, finished 4th. Wetmore quote: "He's been snakebit by interruptions the last three years and he feels that he has something to prove this season."
  - **Tom Reese**: senior team captain, recovering from arthroscopic knee surgery (May) atop a healed cracked femur from years prior. 1997: 33rd at NCAA's, All-American.
  - **Adam Batliner**: 5th-year senior, 1997: 26th at NCAA XC, 3rd in steeplechase at outdoor NCAA's despite training intermittently due to a fibula stress fracture.
  - **Mike Friedberg**: sophomore, walk-on from The Park School (Baltimore, MD — "hardly a recruiting hotbed"), JV runner the year before, now positioned to contend for All-American. Wetmore nickname: "the Iceberg" for unflappable temperament.
  - **Matt Napier**: senior, former walk-on, "one of the great blue-collar success stories of CU running," 1997: 39th at NCAA's after 2nd at Mountain Regional.
  - **Oscar Ponce**: junior, Denver North HS (inner-city, no running pedigree) — explicit framing as "the first All-American ever to graduate from Denver North," described as someone whose story is meant "to inspire others, for whom mere survival is a struggle."
  - **Ronald Roybal**: junior, Pojoaque, NM. 1997: 45th at NCAA's after an 80th-place Mountain Regional — Wetmore: "Ronald had one good day of running all fall... when we needed it. Without his breakthrough performance, CU would not have finished third."
  - **Steve Slattery**: true freshman, NJ state XC champion, 4th at Foot Locker Nationals — Wetmore predicts he will likely redshirt.
  - **Chris Severy**: senior, battled mononucleosis the year before, 17th at NCAA's two years prior.
  - **Brock Tessman**: grad transfer from Brown, national-caliber 1500m runner.

## Introduction (pp. 1-4) — Nov 23, 1998, Lawrence, KS, NCAA Championship morning

- Frames the whole book: Goucher has chased the NCAA XC title since finishing 2nd as a true freshman in 1994 (highest freshman NCAA finish since Bob Kennedy in 1988).
- **Wetmore's pre-race words to Goucher**: "You're fine, Adam. You're fine. Trust it, Adam. Trust all your work." — a real, direct quote; usable as a coaching-communication example (calm, repetition, appeal to trust-in-preparation rather than new instruction moments before a race).
- **No-splits policy**: Wetmore tells the team before the NCAA race: "Gentlemen, you'll hear no splits, and you won't see any mile markers. You're running by feel. Pay attention to your sensory data." This visibly unsettles at least one runner (Friedberg) despite months of training — teammate Jay Johnson talks him down: "Don't worry about this... This is fake. What's real is Magnolia Road, what's real is miles out on the course." **Knowledge unit candidate: racing-by-feel philosophy + the specific psychological technique of a veteran reframing race-day novelty/anxiety by pointing back to the concrete, already-banked training work as "what's real."**
- Race context: 31 teams, 250 runners. Rival Northern Arizona in adjacent box; Oregon (primary rival) at far end.
- Goucher's necklace of shrunken skulls as a personal ritual/psychological prop ("symbols of his prey... determined to add more scalps").
- A specific rival narrative: Kenyan Julius Mwangi (Butler) beat Goucher on this same Lawrence course Oct 10 (in-season meet), celebrated exuberantly; Goucher's quoted reaction: "I'll destroy him when I'm ready." (Tier 4 — colorful but book-specific bravado, low standalone value beyond illustrating competitive psychology / rivalry-as-motivator.)

## "94 Days to Lawrence" (pp. 5-8) — Aug 18, 1998, first practice

- **Team size**: 23 men (roster). First practice at Balch Field House, 3:15pm.
- **"In singles" — a named Wetmore/Lydiard-lineage training concept** (explicit footnote in the book, p.5): *"'In singles' is a common term in Wetmore-speak. He is not overly concerned with volume. A Lydiard disciple, he stresses the greater physiological benefit of running, for example, 70 minutes in one run as opposed to two runs that total 70 minutes."* — **High-value, directly citable, explicitly-named principle: consolidated single runs preferred over split/double runs of equal total time, credited explicitly to Lydiard's influence.** Goucher's summer: 95 miles in one week, entirely in singles, while working a 50-hour/week job at the Olympic Training Center + a dog track, rising ~6:30am to run.
- **Concrete summer-training data point (Goucher, pre-season, Colorado Springs, ~7500 ft elevation)**: ran a 5-mile "AT" (anaerobic threshold — Wetmore's own term/abbreviation, used repeatedly) in 25:43 (~5:09/mile) during a 95-mile week. Same week, other CU runners (Severy, Ponce, Tessman, Schafer, + freshman Slattery on his first day at altitude) ran a 5-mile AT at Potts Field (CU track): Severy/Friedberg ran 26:30; Slattery pulled off after 2 miles in 10:40, saying "I feel like crap. I thought I was in shape, but that was tough!" — used narratively to establish altitude's immediate, humbling physiological effect on an unacclimatized newcomer.
- **First team workout structure**: 10-16 x loops of Kittredge Field (flat, half-mile-circumference grass field) with a 200m "pickup" at cross-country race pace each lap, done "by feel" (no watches). Coaches place cones to mark the 200m segment.
- Wetmore's explicit reasoning for building conservatively into the first hard efforts: after the workout, warning the group ahead of the next day's 13-mile Flagstaff run: "Careful what you're doing, Flagstaff tomorrow." And closing line: "We got ninety-four days to go. We don't need to show off." — **Knowledge unit: coach explicitly manages athlete pacing/restraint in early-season workouts by naming the long horizon of the season ("94 days") rather than the workout in front of them — a patience-over-single-session-ego framing.**
- Batliner's status: "arguably the best shape of his life," an 18-mile run the prior Sunday capping a 94-mile week, described as "the culmination of eight weeks of great base training."
- Reese's return-from-injury framing: hadn't run until Sept 23 two years prior (injury), maxed at 40 miles/week that year, finished 60th at NCAA's; Wetmore's plan for 1998 has him targeting seven 85-mile weeks before NCAA's if all goes perfectly. Reese quote on ambition, explicitly modest/relative rather than title-focused: "I want Nordberg [Friedberg] to do well, top five at NCAA's. I just want to be one step ahead [of Friedberg]." — **Individualization/psychology data point: a senior former-injured runner frames his own goal not as an absolute time/place but as staying just ahead of a specific teammate — internal team competition as a personal motivator distinct from the team's stated NCAA-title goal.**

## "Like Ten Pounds of Shit in a Five-Pound Bag" (pp. 9-11) — Aug 19, 1998, Flagstaff Mountain run

- **The Flagstaff Mountain long run — a named, recurring CU staple workout**: from Balch gym up to Chautauqua Park, then a single-track trail to the summit of Flagstaff Mountain (Boulder, CO). Climbs 1,400+ ft over ~4 miles to the summit (~30 min climb), levels off briefly at the summit, then descends via a different fire trail for several more miles to Red Lion Inn on Canyon Road. **Total run: 13 miles.** Explicitly prescribed as "easy and conversational" pace by Wetmore despite the terrain (veterans visibly skeptical this is achievable on this course). Main pack's time: Flagstaff summit in 1:21, plus a campus loop, for 90 minutes total.
- Batliner's colorful description of the run's difficulty curve: "The thirteen feels like eight because you work your ass off, you coast back home." Wes Berkshire, after doing the climb plus an add-on loop: "feels like ten pounds of shit in a five-pound bag." Both are Tier 4 color but reinforce a real Tier-1 point: **terrain-driven perceived effort can diverge sharply from stated/prescribed intensity** — a genuinely "easy pace" run on steep, technical terrain still produces a hard physiological/psychological experience, which is a real coaching communication challenge (prescribing effort vs. pace on variable terrain).
- Two transfers (O'Mara, Tessman) get lost on their first Flagstaff run after teammate Robbie's IT band flares up and he can't lead them down the correct fire trail — they retrace their steps and arrive back on campus 1:45 after departure (vs. 90 min for the main group). **Minor but real logistical/coaching lesson: unfamiliar, technical trail terrain is a genuine risk factor for new team members, independent of fitness — course-familiarity has to be taught, not assumed.**
- Introduces the roster-security stakes for walk-ons: Chris Valenti and Jason Robbie both must "earn their spot" at a Varsity Time Trial 17 days later (Sept 5) — described explicitly as "a subjective decision on Wetmore's part." **Coaching-practice note: even for a walk-on with prior team experience (Valenti made the squad the year before), roster spots are not carried over automatically; a fresh time trial performance is required every year.**

## "The Unspoken Agreement" (pp. 12-15) — Aug 20, 1998, team training camp, Buena Vista, CO

- **Camp described as explicitly training-focused, not social**: "There will be no booze, no panty raids, and no respite from the training." Buena Vista sits at "an unforgiving elevation of over 7000 feet" — notably higher than Boulder's own already-high 5,500 ft, deliberately stacking additional altitude stress onto camp for a squad already altitude-based year-round.
- **Wetmore's own stated purpose for camp** (direct quote): "the weekend 'provides an opportunity for interpersonal intimacy few and far between once we're in our normal routine.'" And: camp is "his opportunity to create a rapport between the staff and the athletes that will make work and trust easier down the road." Explicitly frames the goal as creating "a *synergy* among the members... at season's end, the sum of the parts will be greater than the whole." — **High-value, directly-citable coaching-culture principle: a coach explicitly using a low-stakes, non-training camp setting as deliberate team-culture/trust infrastructure, not just a training block.**
- **A specific, deliberate coaching technique: athlete-set goals, not coach-imposed goals.** At the camp's team-only closed-door meeting (captains-led, coaches absent), the seniors set the season's objectives themselves rather than being handed a mandate by Wetmore. The book states this explicitly as calculated: *"It is ingenious of Wetmore that he does not dictate what they will be shooting for, but that he lets them decide what they hope to achieve. It is their choice. Once they elect to aim high, they are effectively empowering Wetmore to train them as hard as he sees fit to take them to that level. If they complain, he has but to remind them that they are the ones who decided they wanted to excel."* — **This is one of the single highest-value coaching-principle extractions in the book so far: goal-setting ownership transferred to athletes converts a coach's demanding program from something imposed to something consented-to, and pre-empts athlete complaints about training difficulty by anchoring back to the athletes' own stated ambition.**
- Captain Tom Reese opens the meeting with a quote: "If you're not enjoying the journey, you probably won't enjoy the destination." Captain Heather Burroughs (women's team) reframes the meeting's real purpose bluntly: "Mark wants to know what our priorities are."
- **Batliner on team culture, direct quote**: "We're not here for training, we're here to hang out and be friends, because friendship is the foundation of our team. When the foundation is friendship, it makes it a hell of a lot easier to get through the season." — team-culture/cohesion-as-performance-infrastructure principle.
- **Goucher on Wetmore's autonomy-supportive coaching style, direct quote** (from a post-meeting comment, presented as block quote in book): *"Mark gives us the decision. Hey, if you want to go out on Saturday and party and drink, fine, practice is at 8 a.m. Most people here want to be the best at what we do. We've chosen. We don't want to live a lifestyle of partying. Maybe that's why we fit so well with Mark. He doesn't want to party either. He encourages us to make our own decisions in running and in life... That's why this team is so righteous outside of running."* — direct, attributable, high-value coaching/culture quote (autonomy-supportive coaching, natural consequences rather than rules-enforcement).
- Freshman Aaron Blondeau introduced: Salida, CO native, accustomed to altitude/mountain trails unlike most incoming teammates, 4.0 GPA computer-science major, W.W.J.D. bracelet, 12th at Foot Locker Nationals in HS, only freshman to make CU's top 7 the year before (101st at NCAA's as a true freshman), 7th at US Junior Championships, made the US Junior World team but a shin stress fracture forced him to miss Worlds. Redshirted the following spring. **Individualization data point: rare case of a true freshman making a team's varsity 7 immediately, contrasted elsewhere in the roster with several redshirt/multi-year-development cases (Napier, Reese, Friedberg).**
