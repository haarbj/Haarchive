## Working framework

A personal three-phase model for racing a 5K, taught by Brody's own coach, distinct from (but related to) Jack Daniels' two-phase "brain, then heart" framing already covered in `the-philosophy-of-running`:

1. **Mind** — the first third. Hold the planned pace against a field that's already surging. This is where judgment does the work: resisting the pull of an overly fast start.
2. **Body** — the middle third. Deliberately switch off conscious pace-monitoring and let a well-trained rhythm carry the effort — "falling asleep" on your feet. Not disengagement for its own sake: it's a conservation move. Monitoring pace, form, and the field every step burns real mental energy, and there's only so much of it to spend over three fast miles.
3. **Spirit** — the final third. This is *not* the brain switching off. Closing well still takes real mental effort — just a different kind than the bookkeeping of phase one: less monitoring, more resolve. The point of conserving mental energy in the body phase is to arrive at the spirit phase with enough of it left to actually use — to mentally lock in.

## The refinement to emphasize when this gets written up

The easiest way for a reader to misread this framework is to think "spirit" or "heart" means switching the brain off entirely for the last third. That's wrong, and worth stating explicitly in the eventual article: the body phase's whole purpose is to *bank* mental energy, not spend it, so there's something left to spend deliberately at the end. Heart isn't the absence of thought — it's closer to the *why*: the reason the remaining effort is worth spending at all, once the legs alone aren't enough to get there on their own.
