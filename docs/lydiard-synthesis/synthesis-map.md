source file. Within each section: a priority tier (P1 = clearly warrants a
new heading/callout, P2 = worth folding in as an added citation/example on
an existing point, P3 = skip or mention only in passing) and the citation to
use per the `existing-coverage.md` convention.

## 1. `how-to-start-running` (Getting Started — thin, 1 section, priority home)

**P1 — new content:**
- Sheehan's "How fast/far/often" framework + Borg 6-20 RPE scale (a
  genuinely different, more precise self-regulation tool than the site's
  current "pleasantly tired" language) — `(Sheehan, Jogging Basics)`.

  fit before their joints/tendons are ready.

**P2 — reinforces existing "pleasantly tired" coverage:**
- Sheehan's "train, don't strain," "pace vs. speed" distinction, Bowerman's
  talk-test — minor reinforcement, use only if it tightens prose rather
  than pads it.

**P3 — skip:** First Steps' RunningWizard.com plan-tier promotional content.

## 2. `exercise-physiology` (The Science — already dense, be selective)

**P1 — genuinely new physiology, not on the site:**
- Fuel-source-by-fiber-type + full metabolic equations (2 vs. 36 vs. 130 ATP
  per glucose/fat pathway) — `(Livingstone, HITSYSTEM Clinic Slides)`.
- Eccentric vs. concentric cardiac hypertrophy, with the two real journal
  citations (Lewis et al. 2012, *Arc Bras Cardiol* 2018) — the single
  strongest new scientific claim in the whole folder.
- "Deflection point" / threshold-speed terminology, lactate ~4mmol/L
  threshold marker — nuances the existing oxygen-debt-ceiling material with
  vocabulary the site currently lacks.
- Lactate-not-a-toxin reframing (lactate as fuel/buffer, not waste) — from
  both the Jogging: The Lydiard Way editorial layer and the "Intolerable
  Acidosis" diagram; genuinely updates the site's current framing.
- Capillarization/angiogenesis mechanism (why long aerobic runs build
  capillary density) — mechanistic explanation the site's existing
  Swedish-vs-Kenyan study doesn't provide.

**P2 — adds a number to something already covered:**
- Corrected oxygen-debt ceiling: the editorial layer in *Jogging: The
  Lydiard Way* says "~5 liters" vs. the site's existing "~4 liters" (via
  Snell) — **flag this discrepancy explicitly in the prose** rather than
  silently picking one; both trace to the same Snell-correction lineage.
- VO2max defined as max sustainable pace for 8-11 minutes.
- Two lactate turn-points shifting rightward with training (Colin
  Livingstone diagrams) — precise but doesn't require new prose, works as a
  tightened citation.

**P3 — skip:** granular RER/biochemistry beyond what's useful to a
non-specialist reader; the fiber contraction-power-vs-load chart (too
in-the-weeds for this page's voice).

## 6. `marathon-training` (Coaching & Training)

**P1:**
- Peter Snell's granular, dated 1961-62 training log building to his 1:44.3
  800m WR (via the Livingstone excerpt in the Moller *Running Times*
  article) — an exceptional, highly specific real-world example. Note:
  it's an 800m buildup, not a marathon, but the marathon-as-conditioning
  device is the through-line (the Owairaka Marathon mid-buildup, "clean
  bowled" cricket anecdote) that ties it to this page.
- Lydiard's own 1962 *Sports Illustrated* article: "miles make the
  champions," the reverse-periodization algorithm (10-day taper ← 6wk
  coordination ← 4wk anaerobic ← 4wk hills ← conditioning), 220-yard
  sharpening reps (30→25 sec), and the marathon-racing-frequency detail.
  `(Lydiard, "Why I Prescribe Marathons for Milers," Sports Illustrated,
  1962)`.
- The "Other Views of the Lydiard Way" sidebar (Elliott, Cerutty, Igloi,
  Bannister, Stampfl, Bowerman, etc.) — a rare chance to show contemporary
  disagreement in named experts' own words. Good material for a "coaches
  disagreed, then and now" framing rather than presenting Lydiard's method
  as universally endorsed.

**P2:**
- Full specific weekly mileage schedule with effort levels (Mon-Sun, ~102
  mi/week) from the Lydiard Method Presentation — one good concrete example
  if the page wants a sample schedule.
- Lydiard's own skepticism of weight training for distance runners ("I'd
  bet Halberg does none at all") — a genuine internal-tension flag against
  the site's existing favorable strength-training coverage; worth a
  one-line acknowledgment rather than silently omitting.

## 7. `nutrition-and-fueling` (Recovery & Fueling — thin-ish, good home)

**P1 — the folder's richest nutrition material, but needs careful framing
since it's a real *counter-narrative* to the site's existing honey/carb-load
Lydiard stance:**
- Tim Noakes publicly disavowing his own *Lore of Running* carb chapter —
  a named, credentialed authority reversing course; strong "expert
  disagreement, stated honestly" material.
- FASTER study fat-oxidation numbers (low-carb elites ~1.54 g/min vs.
  high-carb ~0.67 g/min) and the specific elite-athlete case studies (Attia,
  Inkinen, Bitter) — concrete, well-sourced.
- RED-S replacing "Female Athlete Triad" (Mountjoy et al. 2014) — a real
  medical-framework update the site doesn't have at all; include the
  energy-availability formula and recovery timeline.
- The 80%/50% fat-capacity-by-training-status figure (Costill 1979, via
  Livingstone) — extends the existing capillary/fuel material with a
  specific, well-sourced number.

**P2:**
- Lydiard's own extensive race-day checklist and micronutrient rundown
  (from the 1999 lecture-tour guide) — mostly reinforces existing honey/
  salt-tablet coverage with more specifics (8oz honey, lubricant, lacing),
  but the vitamin C claims are dubious (detoxifies "heroin, nicotine,
  alcohol") and should be **explicitly excluded**, not cited as fact.

**P3 — skip:** the general US obesity/diabetes epidemiology (not
running-specific), the telomere/omega-3/statin/earthing material (health

**Framing note for Phase 3:** this section should present the low-carb
material as a genuine, credentialed-but-contested alternative view next to
the existing honey/carb-load Lydiard stance — not as a replacement. Several
digests flag Cucuzzella/Defty/Noakes explicitly as *not* Lydiard's own
position.

## 9. New/reconsidered: running technique and footwear philosophy

**This is the folder's single biggest *coverage gap* finding** — technique
and shoe philosophy do not exist anywhere on the site right now, and three
independent sources (Cucuzzella's slides, René Borg's blog, and Lydiard's
own quotes threaded through both) converge on the same well-documented
Lydiard positions:

- "Sitting in a bucket" / running tall (posture cue).
- "Thud versus Spring" (heel-strike vs. elastic forefoot loading).
- Canvas-shoe history, "second layer of skin," "trainers vs. racers,"
  skepticism of expensive hi-tech shoes, rejection of orthotics ("orthotics
  are for the shoes, not the feet").
- Technique-before-energy-systems sequencing (a real nuance against
  Lydiard's popular reputation as purely an "aerobic volume" coach).
- Sebastian Coe video-technique anecdote.

**Recommendation:** this is dense and coherent enough to justify a genuinely
new subsection — either a new heading inside `workout-library` (technique
sits closest to "how you train," not physiology or history) or, if it grows
large enough once drafted, a case for a small new standalone section. Given
`coaching-and-training` is already the site's largest category (8 sections),
lean toward a new heading inside `workout-library` rather than a new page.

## Cross-cutting notes for Phase 3

1. **Discrepancy to flag, not silently resolve:** the oxygen-debt-ceiling
   correction is cited as "~4 liters" in existing site content and "~5
   liters" in the Hashizume & Moller editorial layer — both trace to the
   same post-Lydiard correction lineage (Snell et al.), so Phase 3 should
   either cite a range ("~4-5 liters") or note the figure varies by source
   rather than asserting a single precise number.
2. **Genuine expert disagreement to preserve, not flatten:** Lydiard himself
   was skeptical of weight training for distance runners, in tension with
   the site's existing favorable heavy-low-rep-lifting coverage (itself
   drawn from Livingstone). Also: the low-carb/keto nutrition material
   (Cucuzzella, Defty, Noakes) is a real counter-position to Lydiard's own
   honey/carb-loading stance — both should be presented as "different
   camps," not merged into one voice.
3. **Duplicate source across batches:** the Costill 1979 fat-capacity chart
   (via Livingstone) appears independently in batch-05, batch-11, and
   batch-12 with slightly different numbers each time (50%→80% vs.
   20%/80%) — reconcile to one number when drafting, cite once.
4. **Low-value/skip entirely:** general US public-health epidemiology,
   personal health-memoir material (statins, telomeres, earthing,
   Cucuzzella's own blood panels), and the extensive Auckland-club/masters-
   roster trivia beyond a name or two.
