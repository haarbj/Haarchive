### Personal anecdotes / self-experimentation (author's own data, offered as case study)

- **[OVERLAP]** -- different take on marathon fueling. Cucuzzella describes
  his own before/after: in 2006 (age 40) he was "uberfit but not healthy"
  running on a high-carb diet ("Burning Bagels"), with fasting blood glucose
  in the 120s and triglycerides >200/HDL in the 40s; by 2011 (age 45) on a
  lower-carb, fat-adapted diet ("Burning Butter") he was "a little less fit
  but healthier," with improving labs. By 2019 he reports HDL 106, LDL 65,
  TG 67, A1C down from 6.3 to 5.5, clean inflammatory markers, and a zero
  coronary artery calcium (CAC) score, alongside running Boston Marathon
  2018. This directly complicates the existing marathon-fueling coverage:
  it's a first-person argument that a real-food/lower-carb approach can
  preserve endurance performance while improving metabolic health, a
  different frame from the site's existing honey-vs-carb-loading discussion.
- **[NOVEL]** Race results offered as proof-points for this fueling
  approach: Marine Corps Marathon 2016, 2:55:26 (1st in division, 81st
  overall); JFK 50 Mile ultramarathon, Nov 19, 2016 (photos/finish, no time
  given in this page range).
- **[NOVEL]** Concrete pre-race/on-course fueling protocol for a 50-mile
  ultra, explicitly framed as answering "Do you need to 'Carb Up'?":
  breakfast of natural peanut butter (Justin's), Generation UCAN (a
  slow-release starch supplement), a banana, and Vespa (a wasp-venom-derived
  fat-metabolism supplement popular in ultrarunning); on-course carried
  UCAN packets, a water bottle, and more Vespa. Named product/brand-level
  specificity not present in existing coverage's marathon-fueling section.
- **[NOVEL]** Anecdote about a patient/friend "DT": 3 months prior, weight
  600+ lbs, A1c 15, creatinine 7, an ICU patient; "today," weight 530 lbs,
  A1c 5.5, on metformin only, discharged from renal clinic with normal
  renal function and "energy back." Offered as a dramatic real-food/
  lifestyle-intervention case study, not running-specific.
