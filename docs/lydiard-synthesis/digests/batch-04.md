### Extracted bullets

- **[NOVEL]** 2018 US HHS Physical Activity Guidelines Advisory Committee report (cited as external authority, not Lydiard-specific): recommends 150+ min/week moderate-to-vigorous activity; running is explicitly classified as "vigorous" alongside walking 5+ mph, biking 10+ mph, and steady-lap swimming. Also cited: 50% of Americans don't meet the 150-300 min/week recommendation, and 30% report no physical activity at all. Low relevance to Lydiard method itself — general public-health framing device used to open the clinic.

- **[NOVEL]** "Old School is the Best School" slide argues several timeless fitness texts (Bowerman's *Jogging*, Cooper's *Aerobics*, Lydiard's *Running to the Top*, Lydiard/Gilmour's *Run for Your Life*) share a common prescription: **30 minutes a day as the minimum dose, building up to it**.

- **[NOVEL]** "Added Benefits" claim: benefit accrues from *any* addition of non-sedentary activity — going from sitting all day to 5 minutes of walking provides measurable health benefit, and 5→10 minutes continues to add benefit. Framed as a "just take the first step" argument for absolute beginners.

- **[NOVEL]** Lydiard Foundation's codified **"Five Principles of the Lydiard Method"** (a distinct branded curriculum framework not identical to anything already on the site): (1) Aerobic Base Development, (2) Response-Regulated™ Training, (3) Feeling-Based Training, (4) Sequential Program, (5) Correct Timing. This is a modern packaging/terminology layer, not verbatim Lydiard.

- **[NOVEL]** Detailed aerobic/anaerobic energy-system contribution table by event distance (data credited to Peter Coe & Dave Martin's *Training Distance Runners*), giving % VO2max and % energy from phosphate/lactate/aerobic pathways:
  - 100m: 70% phosphate / 22% lactate / 8% aerobic
  - 200m: 40 / 46 / 14
  - 400m: 10 / 60 / 30
  - 800m (135% VO2max): 5 / 38 / 57
  - 1500m (112% VO2max): 2 / 22 / 76
  - 3000m (102% VO2max): <1 / 12 / 88
  - 5000m (97% VO2max): <1 / 7 / 93
  - 10,000m (92% VO2max): <1 / 3 / 97
  - Marathon (82% VO2max): <1 / <1 / 99
  This is a specific numeric breakdown not present in the existing aerobic/anaerobic or steady-state material on the site.

- **[NOVEL]** "Mighty Chondria" claim: improving mitochondrial density/function may make a person "leaner, more energetic, have less disease, and live longer" — a longevity/health claim tied to mitochondrial adaptation, beyond the existing site's fiber-type coverage.

- **[NOVEL]** Memorable coaching metaphor: "Is your training Pisa or Giza?" — building an aerobic base is like building a castle on rock (sturdy foundation, room to keep improving); skipping the aerobic base for race-specific work is "like building a sandcastle on the beach — regardless of how grand it may look, it'll crumble down with a few waves."

- **[OVERLAP — overtraining/recovery balance]** Supercompensation model: workout stress temporarily *decreases* fitness, recovery lets it "rebound" beyond the prior level (= supercompensation = the training effect). Chart gives specific timing: **2 hours of training stress, ~10 hours of recovery drawdown, supercompensation peaking around the 24-hour mark** (glycogen-depletion/repletion curve). This numeric timing detail is new; the underlying stress-recovery-adaptation concept overlaps with the site's existing overtraining/recovery material.

- **[OVERLAP — overtraining symptom clusters]** "Three types of training mistakes" framing (new terminology, same underlying idea already covered): (1) too much workload, (2) too little workload, (3) inadequate recovery. Also reinforced: too-light workload/too-long recovery → no fitness gain; too-demanding workload/inadequate recovery → progressively declining fitness and chronic fatigue.

- **[NOVEL]** Explicit claim: "The cardiovascular system develops more quickly than the musculo-skeletal system" (illustrated as a heart character outrunning a skeleton saying "Hey! Wait for me!"). This is a distinct, quotable framing of a real training-progression principle (cardiovascular fitness outpaces structural/tendon/bone adaptation) not stated this way elsewhere on the site — useful for explaining why beginners feel "fit enough" before their joints/tendons are ready and get injured.

- **[OVERLAP — beginner "pleasantly tired" philosophy]** Third principle, "Feeling-Based Training — learning the language of your own body": internal feedback systems, once developed, become "the most reliable central reference point for gauging optimal training." Technological monitoring devices (HR monitors, etc.) are useful for beginners to calibrate, but explicit caution: "do not become so reliant on them that you are bypassing your own inner technology." This nuances the site's existing beginner philosophy with an explicit stance on wearables/tech that isn't there yet.

- **[NOVEL]** Fourth principle, "Sequential Development," presents a specific **24-week Peak Performance Pyramid** block breakdown: Health (base, undated) → Aerobic Conditioning 10 weeks → Hills 4 weeks → Anaerobic 4 weeks → Integration/Coordination 4 weeks → Taper 2 weeks = 24 weeks total. The individual blocks (hills, taper) are covered elsewhere on the site, but this specific consolidated week-count table is new.

- **[NOVEL]** Fifth principle, "Correct Timing," memorable direct quote: **"More is Less and Less is More!"** — framed as the essential skill of knowing the right training type at the right time, how long to hold a training type for maximum effect, when to push vs. pull back, in-run pacing, and race peaking.

- **[NOVEL]** Nested "Timing Considerations" hierarchy (terminology framework): Long-term Development ⊃ Goal ⊃ Phase ⊃ Weekly Schedule ⊃ Daily Session ⊃ Stimulus (workout or recovery session).

- **[NOVEL]** Direct quote, explicitly framed as a misconception-correction: training should be **"Response Regulated by the individual athlete — not Number Regulated."** This directly counters the common misreading of Lydiard as a rigid mileage-number system, tying together aerobic capacity → anaerobic capacity → speed/economy-of-action as the sequential development order.

- **[OVERLAP — 1961 Auckland jogging club]** New biographical detail: Lydiard was **President of the Auckland Joggers Club from 1962 to 2004** (i.e., held the role until his death), not just its 1961 founding figure. Also new: the club's **"Round the Bays" fun-run started in 1972**, explicitly framed as evidence NZ was "already jogging en masse" well before Bowerman brought jogging to America.

- **[NOVEL]** Anecdote: "Lydiard — the first man to get cardiac patients jogging/running." Names a specific patient, **Andy Steadman, 76 years old, with 3 coronaries** (heart attacks), pictured jogging. Direct quote attributed to Lydiard: **"The heart's just another muscle which needs to get fit with exercise."** This is a specific named case history and quote not present in the existing historical jogging-movement coverage (which has Halberg, the 1961 club, Bowerman's visit, and the East German program, but not this cardiac-rehab anecdote).

- **[OVERLAP — Bowerman's 1962 visit/founding of American jogging]** New details and a date discrepancy: this deck dates the Bowerman connection to **1967**, not 1962. New terminology distinction given: **"New Zealand Fartlek"** = slow steady run/walk, vs. **"Swedish Fartlek"** = "speed play... you can play, fool around in this workout." New Lydiard quote: **"Train, Don't Strain."** New anecdote/photo caption: "Bowerman at 50 couldn't keep up with the Lydiard-trained coronary patients."

- **[OVERLAP — capillary-density study]** "The Capillary System" slide adds a specific, quotable figure: the capillary network is a **"60,000 Mile Network"** delivering oxygen/glucose and removing waste from working muscle — a striking total-length number not in the existing capillary-density (Swedish vs. Kenyan) coverage.

- **[OVERLAP — fast/slow-twitch fiber physiology]** "Why Easy Running/Jogging" rationale: easy running gently strengthens muscles/joints/tendons/cardiovascular system for harder work later, "capillarizes" running muscles, and increases both oxidative enzyme activity and **mitochondrial population specifically in slow-twitch muscle fibers**. Also: correct endurance training raises the body's ability to "run aerobically at fast speeds" by developing the cardiac system, capillary/vascular system, mitochondrial density/oxidative enzymes, and fuel systems — specifically **utilizing more fat at higher intensities than before, sparing carbohydrate stores**. This fat-sparing-at-higher-intensity claim is a nuance beyond what's currently on the site.

- **[NOVEL]** Cardiovascular training-adaptation list with a concrete comparative image: heart muscle strengthens, stroke volume increases, resting HR decreases, heart does more work in fewer beats, total work capacity increases, and "the deflection point increases" (referencing the Conconi heart-rate deflection point / lactate-threshold marker — new terminology for the site). Includes an X-ray comparison image (credited to Dr. Dave Costill's *A Scientific Approach to Distance Running*) showing heart width **10.2 cm in a normal 45-year-old vs. 15.3 cm in a 45-year-old marathoner** — a specific, citable cardiac-hypertrophy number.
