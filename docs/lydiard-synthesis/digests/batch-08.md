### Framework and terminology

- **[NOVEL]** Core HIIT concept: breaking a work period into several bouts with brief recoveries lets an athlete accumulate a *larger overall stimulus* than performing the same work as one continuous bout.
- **[NOVEL]** HIIT prescription is a balancing act between the aerobic side (O2 delivery, fractional utilization) and the anaerobic side (power, capacity).
- **[NOVEL]** Nine-variable framework for describing any interval prescription (credited to a "Periodization Training / Swimming Puzzle" source, pp. 336–338): work modality, work intensity, work duration, relief intensity, relief duration, # of series, series duration, time between series, between-series recovery intensity.
- **[NOVEL]** Named interval session taxonomy with numeric ranges:
  - "Traditional" long intervals: work bout 2–15 min, >lactate-threshold intensity, 60–180s recovery.
  - "Micro-interval blocks": work 10s–60s, rest 5s–60s, often grouped 2–4 work-blocks per series with 3–5 min rest between series.
  - Repeated Sprint Training (RST): sprints <10s, recoveries 30–60s.
  - Sprint Interval Training (SIT): typically 4–8 maximal 30-second bouts, 3–5 min recovery.
- **[NOVEL]** "Isoeffort" model (Sjursen, credited alongside Seiler): matching interval sessions by target training *effort*, not just by total work duration or intensity — used as the design principle across several of the studies below.
- **[NOVEL]** Closing coaching-cue summary ("Athletes solve the prescription equation"):
  - Work-bout duration and total accumulated work duration are the two variables that push/pull an athlete into the desired intensity zone.
  - Rest-interval duration has little added effect once it's above ~90 seconds — but adding work intensity *during* rest periods (i.e., fartlek) brings work-bout intensity down quickly. **[OVERLAP — Fartlek-by-time]**: this is a physiological rationale for fartlek-style continuous-variation training, which the site already covers via Lydiard's fartlek-by-time material; this deck adds the mechanistic "why" (rest-period intensity dilutes work-bout intensity) rather than new numbers.
  - Prescribe athletes to hold constant power/pace throughout a session ("maximal session effort") rather than let effort taper.
  - Let heart rate float within the prescription; don't use HR recovery to gate rest duration — just fix rest at ~120 seconds.
  - Lower intensity + higher total accumulated work duration can give equal or greater fitness gain for less "pain" than shorter/harder intervals.

### Physiological data — historical exercise-physiology studies cited

- **[NOVEL]** Åstrand et al., *Acta Physiol. Scand.* 48:448, 1960 — cycle ergometer, fixed 1-hour workload comparison: continuous 175W (VO2 2.44 L/min, HR 134, blood lactate 1.3mM) vs. continuous 350W to exhaustion in 9 min (ventilation 124 L/min, HR 190, lactate 16.7mM) vs. the *same* 350W total work broken into intervals — 30s/30s work/rest cut lactate to 2.2mM, 60s/60s to 5.0mM, 2min/2min to 10.5mM, 3min/3min to 13.3mM. Demonstrates that breaking identical total work into shorter work/rest cycles dramatically lowers physiological cost.
- **[NOVEL]** Christiansen et al., *Acta Physiol. Scand.* 50:269, 1960 — one subject running 30 min on a treadmill at 20 km/h using different work:rest combinations (continuous pace sustainable only 4 min to exhaustion, VO2max 5.6 L/min, ventilation 156 L/min, lactate 16.7mM). Intermittent variants at the same pace: 5-5s (avg VO2 4.3 L/min, lactate 2.6mM), 10-5s (peak VO2 5.6, lactate 4.9mM), 10-10s (VO2 4.4, lactate 2.2mM), 15-30s (VO2 3.6, lactate 1.8mM) — shows how sensitive physiological cost is to the work:rest ratio even at identical running speed.

### Physiological data — Seiler's own and colleagues' published research

- **[NOVEL]** Seiler & Sjursen, *Scand. J. Med. Sci. Sports* 14:318-325, 2004 — compared 24×1min, 12×2min, 6×4min, 4×6min (all 1:1 work:rest, "maximal session effort"): %VO2max plateaus around 90-100% for 2/4/6-min bouts but only ~72-90% for 1-min bouts; RPE climbs roughly linearly with accumulated interval duration regardless of individual bout length. The deck flags 6×4min as the favored/checked prescription.
- **[NOVEL]** Same dataset, Table 2: peak VO2 by bout duration — 1min: 81.8±5.2% VO2max; 2min: 92.4±4.4%; 4min: 93.3±4.8%; 6min: 91.7±3.0%. VO2 at end of recovery periods drops much further after longer work bouts (46.0% after 1-min bouts vs. 25.6-30.6% after 2/4/6-min bouts), yet average session VO2 stays nearly flat (~59-61%) across all four prescriptions.
- **[NOVEL]** Seiler & Hetlelid, *MSSE* 37(9):1601-1607, 2005 (Ken Hetlelid, 1975–2016) — 6×4min work with 1, 2, or 4 min recovery: RPE rose similarly across the 6 bouts regardless of recovery length. In a 4th session, subjects blinded to time self-selected a "minimum recovery time" needed to complete all 6 intervals at their best average velocity from prior trials — consistently landed at ~110-125 seconds regardless of trial number, suggesting a fairly fixed physiological recovery requirement independent of prescribed rest length.
- **[NOVEL]** Cross-country double-poling ergometer case data: 10×1000m vs. 10×500m at identical 5k power output (208W, ~42 strokes/min) and matched fitness — the longer-interval protocol drove heart rate and physiological strain progressively toward the runner's 5000m time-trial curve, while the shorter-interval protocol stayed well below it despite identical power.
- **[NOVEL]** Micro-interval block example: 20×60s:30s followed by 10×60s:30s of double poling at 110% of 20-min max power, with "effective rest" only 25 seconds once an acceleration requirement is factored in — used to illustrate how micro-interval blocks can still drive HR near-maximal.
- **[NOVEL]** Wåle & Pedersen, Masters research project, University of Agder — "Many short or a few long HIT work bouts" (unpublished data with Seiler & Sylta): 4-week intervention in cyclists comparing LI (4×8min continuous), SI1 (4×[12×40/20s] micro-intervals), SI2 (4×[8×40/20s] micro-intervals), all at matched total session time (32 min). Micro-interval groups achieved significantly higher power output (SI2: 4.3 W/kg vs. LI: 3.7 W/kg, p<0.05), higher %peak power (78% vs. 70%), and higher %Power-40min (115% vs. 104%) and %4mM-lactate-power (116% vs. 104%) — all while RPE (~16.7-16.8) and post-session sRPE (~8.7-8.8) were essentially identical across groups. Training-session power also rose more steeply over 4 weeks in the micro-interval groups than the long-interval group. Despite these differences, the study found **no significant difference in overall training effect** among the three groups after 4 weeks of 3 sessions/week — though the authors speculate specific adaptations to repeated accelerations may not have been captured by the outcome measures used.
- **[NOVEL]** Seiler & Sylta, *Int. J. Sports Physiol. Perform.* 12:1-22, 2017 — 63 well-trained subjects, 3 HIIT prescriptions at maximal session effort (4×16min, 4×8min, 4×4min): power output rose from 276W (4×16) to 308W (4×8) to 342W (4×4); blood lactate rose from 4.7mM to 9.2mM to 12.7mM; RPE from 15.0 to 16.2 to 17.1; and critically, the share of sessions hitting near-maximal peak RPE (19-20/20) jumped from 8.4% (4×16) to 26.8% (4×8) to 60.9% (4×4), with sessions rated 9-10/10 on session-RPE similarly concentrated in the shortest-interval group (32.4% vs. 5.3-7.8%). Demonstrates that matching for "session effort" does not equalize peak physiological/perceptual strain.
- **[NOVEL]** Seiler, Jøransen, Olesen, Hetlelid, *Scand. J. Med. Sci. Sports* 23:74-83, 2013 ("Groupsweat 1.0" study) — 29 cyclists (6 female, VO2max 52±6 mL/kg/min baseline, 6-7h/wk training), randomized to 7 weeks of 2 HIT sessions/week (plus ~7h/wk low-intensity training) using an "iso-effort" prescription: 4×4min/2min rest (Zone 5), 4×8min/2min rest (Zone 4), or 4×16min/3min rest (Zone 3/4), vs. a low-intensity-only control.
  - Session-level data (mean over 7 weeks, 27 trained subjects): 4×16min totaled 98 min work time (73 excl. warm-up) at HR 88±2%, lactate 4.9mM, RPE 15.8, session-RPE 6.8; 4×8min totaled 63 (38) min at HR 90±2%, lactate 9.7mM, RPE 16.4, session-RPE 7.1; 4×4min totaled 47 (22) min at HR 94±2%, lactate 13mM, RPE 18.2, session-RPE 7.8.
  - **[NOVEL]** Key conceptual finding, explicitly flagged in the deck: "**Equivalent session effort ≠ equivalent RPE**" — sessions engineered to produce similar overall training load/effort (sRPE × duration) still produced very different peak/momentary RPE, with the shortest-interval group consistently hitting much higher peak perceived exertion. The deck frames this as raising a question of "exertional pacing."
  - **[NOVEL]** Outcome measures (VO2peak, PowerVO2peak, Power at 4mM lactate, time-to-exhaustion at 80%): the **4×8min group showed the largest median gains across all four measures** — larger than both the higher-intensity 4×4min group and the lower-intensity 4×16min group — an inverted-U "sweet spot" finding, with the 4×4min and 4×16min groups roughly comparable to each other and the low-intensity control trailing all three interval groups.
  - **[NOVEL]** Distribution-of-individual-response analysis: the frequency of subjects showing "large" (>9% improvement) vs. "moderate" vs. "trivial" (<4%) individual responses differed significantly among groups (p<0.05) — the 4×8min and 4×4min groups produced markedly more "large effect" individual responders than the low-intensity or 4×16min groups.
- **[NOVEL]** Trained-vs-well-trained comparison at matched "maximal session effort" prescriptions (4×16, 4×8, 4×4): physiological load (HR-peak %, blood lactate) was nearly identical between the "trained" and "well-trained" groups at each prescription, but the well-trained group reported consistently *lower* RPE and sRPE at the same physiological load (e.g., 4×16min: RPE 15.8 trained vs. 15.0 well-trained; 4×4min: RPE 18.5 trained vs. 17.1 well-trained) — suggesting more experienced/fitter athletes tolerate a given physiological strain with somewhat less perceived effort.
- **[NOVEL]** "HIT intensity vs. tolerable accumulated duration" relationship curve: at ~87% HRmax during work bouts, athletes tolerated ~64 min of accumulated work; at 91% HRmax, ~32 min; at 95% HRmax, ~16 min; at 100% HRmax, ~8 min — an inverse curve the deck explicitly questions ("do all points on this curve have the same training effect or perceived effort?").
- **[NOVEL]** Interval-session HR-drift example (6×4min work, 2min recovery): average session HR 150 bpm (82% of max), average peak working HR 175 bpm (96% of max), average RPE 17, session-RPE 8; work-bout HR drifted up ~10 bpm and recovery-period HR drifted up ~20 bpm over the course of the session at constant running speed — a concrete illustration of cardiovascular drift within a single interval workout.
