# Post-Marathon Recovery Program

*For: a coached athlete in the days after finishing a marathon.
Duration: ~6 weeks (3–5 days off → 2-week return to running → 4-week return to training).
Basis: the program template for Prompt 11, Part H. All paces below are effort-based; where percentages appear they refer to % of the marathon pace you just ran.*

## The arc

The marathon depletes you in two ways. The physical damage — sore, fatigued legs — bounces back fairly quickly if you were well trained. The bigger challenge is mental: a marathon is an enormous bout of sustained concentration, and your motivation and mental energy reserves will be severely depleted afterward. This program is designed around that reality. The early weeks target psychology, not physiology — easy, enjoyable running whose only job is to refill the tank.

One rule for the whole program: **do not take two full weeks of complete rest.** It feels virtuous, but it costs you a non-trivial amount of aerobic fitness and — more importantly — the sudden drop in loading degrades the structural integrity of your musculoskeletal system, which raises injury risk when you start back. Short rest, then gentle motion.

Exception: lingering pain or an injury caused or aggravated by the race. That takes as long as it takes to fully resolve — and many injuries need active rehab, not just rest.

## Phase 0 — Full rest: 3–5 days off

No running. Sleep a little extra, eat real meals, live your life. If you feel great after three days, you can start Phase 1 a bit sooner. There is nothing to gain by forcing extra days off.

## Phase 1 — Return to running (2 weeks)

Begin after at least three days of rest. Every run is very easy to easy, every other day at most. These runs will feel slower and more laborious than you're used to — that is completely normal and completely unimportant. You are not targeting physiology here; you are targeting psychology. Run with friends, run your favorite routes, run somewhere scenic. If the urge strikes to hop into a club workout or a longer trail run, follow it — the psychological benefit outweighs any theoretical cost to physical recovery right now.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 3–4 mi very easy | Off | 4–5 mi very easy | Off | 5 mi very easy | 5–6 mi easy | Off |
| Week 2 | 5–6 mi easy | 4–5 mi light progression | Off or 3–5 mi very easy | 5–7 mi very easy | 4–5 mi easy | 6–8 mi easy to moderate | Off |

## Phase 2 — Return to training (4 weeks)

Begin when you feel ready for structured training — you don't need the full program if time is short, but if you have the time it works as a general introductory phase no matter what's next. Don't obsess over pacing; get the effort right instead. Weekly mileage totals are not critical at this point. For the sessions marked with \*, do a 1–2 mile progressive warm-up and a 1–2 mile easy cool-down.

**Week 1**
- Mon: 5 mi Kenyan-style progression + 1 mi easy
- Tue: 4–5 mi very easy
- Wed: 3 mi easy progressing to moderate, then 8–10 × 20 sec strides
- Thu: 4–5 mi very easy
- Fri: 6–8 mi easy to moderate
- Sat: 5–8 mi very easy
- Sun: Off or 4–5 mi very easy

**Week 2**
- Mon: 6–7 mi easy
- Tue: 6 mi Kenyan-style progression + 1 mi easy
- Wed: 5–7 mi easy
- Thu: 3 mi easy progressing to moderate, then 8–10 × 20 sec strides (3–4 min rest), then 4 min fast (~116% of marathon pace effort), 1 mi easy cool-down
- Fri: 5–7 mi very easy
- Sat: 8–10 mi easy through rolling hills
- Sun: Off or 4–5 mi very easy to easy

**Week 3**
- Mon: 6 mi Kenyan-style progression with fast finish + 1 mi easy
- Tue: 5–8 mi easy
- Wed: 8–12 × 1 min fast uphill (~115% of marathon pace effort), easy jog down (can do on flat ground)
- Thu: 5–8 mi easy
- Fri: 4–5 mi strong at 98–100% of marathon pace \*
- Sat: 5–8 mi very easy
- Sun: 11–12 mi easy through rolling hills

**Week 4**
- Mon: Off or 4–5 mi very easy
- Tue: 5–8 × 3 min at 105% of marathon pace, 45 sec jog \*
- Wed: 5–8 mi very easy
- Thu: 4 km at 110% of marathon pace, 4–5 min walk/jog, then 6–8 × 1 min fast (~115% effort), 2 min jog \*
- Fri: 5–8 mi very easy
- Sat: 6–10 × 1 min fast uphill (~112–115% of marathon pace effort), easy jog down \* (can do on flat ground)
- Sun: Off or 3–5 mi very easy

## Coaching notes

- **Why this shape:** the marathon-specific phase intentionally de-emphasized general fitness (speeds far from marathon pace). Phase 2 rebuilds exactly those qualities — progressions, strides, short hills, moderate running — so the next buildup starts from a complete base.
- **Effort over pace throughout.** The percentages are anchored to the marathon you just ran, but they are guides for effort, not splits to hit.
- **When to move on:** when your legs feel good again and easy running feels like running again, you're done — even if that's before the end of Week 4.
- **What comes next:** three good options — another marathon buildup, a few months focused on a different distance (5K/10K, half marathon), or a period of unstructured training. Do the post-season review questionnaire first, then decide together.
